Experiment No. 7: Double Refraction (Birefringence)

AIM - APPARATUS - PRIOR CONCEPTS

Aim:To determine the refractive index of ordinary and extra-ordinary rays and identification of types of crystal using the double refraction phenomenon.

Apparatus: Spectrometer, source of light (sodium light), quartz prism & polarizer attachments to the telescope.

Prior Concepts: Refraction, double refraction, polarization, Snell's law of refraction.

THEORY

Birefringence is the optical property of a material having a refractive index that depends on the polarization and propagation direction of light, whereby a ray of light, when incident upon a birefringent material, is split by polarization into two rays taking slightly different paths. When a beam of ordinary light i.e. unpolarised light is incident upon a crystal-like calcite or quartz, then upon entering the crystal, the beam splits into two components both of which are plane polarized with their planes of vibrations mutually perpendicular. Thus, corresponding to a single incident ray, there are two refracted rays. The phenomenon is called double refraction or birefringence & the crystal exhibiting this phenomenon is called double refracting crystal.

For example: Double refraction in calcite crystal l.e. Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3): The direction in which the ray of transmitted light does not suffer double refraction inside the crystal is known as the optic axis. One of these beams is found to obey Snell's law of refraction at the crystal Interface & has vibrations perpendicular to the principal section. This beam is known as ordinary ray or O ray. The second beam does not obey Snell's law and has vibrations parallel to the principal section. This beam or ray is called extra ordinary ray or the E ray. For all rays not traveling along the optic axis, the velocity is determined by a pair of refractive indices called the ordinary refractive index 4, and the extraordinary refractive Index. The path of an Incident ray is split into two rays, called O-rays and E-rays. The velocity of O-ray is the same in all directions of a crystal, whereas the E-ray travels with different velocities in different directions.

The velocities of O-ray and E-ray are the same along optic axis. The velocity of E-ray is less than that of O-ray in quartz crystal, this is a positive crystal. The velocity of E-ray is more than that of O-ray in calcite crystal, this is a negative crystal. Along the optic axis, the refractive index ue = uo

Biaxial crystals: In these crystals, there are two directions along which the velocities are same (two optic axis). Examples-Topaz, Aragonite. Positive Crystal: The refractive index muo < mue The velocity vo > ve Example: Quartz (SIO₂), Sellaite (Mg*F2) Rutile (TiO₂). Negative Crystal: The refractive index muo > mue The velocity vo < ve Example: Calcite, Tourmaline, Ruby.
The refractive index for the ordinary and extraordinary rays is calculated from the realation

where A is angle of prism. If the refractive index is uo < ue then the crystal is POSITIVE. If the refractive index is uo > ue then the crystal is NEGATIVE.

PROCEDURE

  1. Initially, by removing the polarizer attachments from the telescope adjust spectrometer for experiment. .
  2. Place the prism on the prism table mount the Polaroid attachment on the telescope objective.
  3. Turn telescope in such way that the light reflected from one of polished face of the prism is received in the telescope.
  4. Adjust the telescope to obtain the reflected light on the cross wire.
  5. Then rotate Polaroid slowly through one complete cycle & observe the variation of intensity of reflected light. The intensity may or may not be zero in one complete rotation of Polaroid.
  6. Repeat step (4) and (5) the number of times by increasing the angle of incidence- till reflected light shows zero intensity.
  7. Record the position of telescope i.e. notes the readings of two verniers.
  8. Then remove the prism from the prism table & turn telescope to get direct image of the slit.
  9. Adjust the image of the slit on the cross wire of the telescope & note readings of two vernier.
  10. Determine the difference of the position of telescope.
